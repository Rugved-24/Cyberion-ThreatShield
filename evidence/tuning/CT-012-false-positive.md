# CT-012 false-positive tuning record

Behaviour: Network logon by a named user account
Shipped rule: `rules/windows/auth_network_logon_interactive_account.yml`
Generated by `scripts/tune.py`. Do not edit the figures by hand.

## Observed false positives

The untuned logic produced 7 matches, of which 6 were attributed benign activity and 1 were the expected attack evidence.

## Change made

Added a filter excluding target accounts whose name ends with the machine-account marker, and a required check that the account name is present at all.

## Before and after

| Measure | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Total matches | 7 | 1 |
| Attributed benign matches | 6 | 0 |
| Expected attack matches | 1 | 1 |
| Known benign group suppressed | 6 | 0 |

## Attack evidence that must survive the change

Declared evidence: DS002:1209.
Attack matches retained after tuning: 1 (DS002:968C4B699F7239D106577B2B8E206EDFAF812F193A60E663966258AF67A56ACC:1209).
Attack matches lost by the change: 0.

## Trade-off accepted

Any attack that authenticates as a machine account is now invisible to this rule. That is an accepted loss: machine-account authentication is dominated by domain replication and service traffic, so keeping those matches would mean giving up the signal entirely. The residual risk is real and is why the playbook asks the analyst to confirm the account owner rather than to trust the alert on its own.

## Observations

- Before: the logon type alone also matched the domain controller's own machine-account logons, which are the majority of type 3 activity there.
- After: only the named account remains, and the type 3 logons for the machine account are no longer reported.
- The benign and the malicious matches are on two different recorded hosts, MORDORDC.theshire.local and WORKSTATION6.theshire.local. The host names are not merged, so this is a comparison of rule logic across two hosts rather than a same-host before and after, and it is described that way in the generated record.

## Comparison population

These recordings are adversary-emulation captures. The records listed here were attributed to ordinary Windows or vendor activity using fields present in the record itself. They are not a separately collected clean environment, they are not exhaustive, and they are not a substitute for one. A client deployment must collect its own baseline before relying on any false-positive rate in this file.
