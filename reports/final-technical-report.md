## 1. Executive summary

Two public adversary-emulation recordings were analysed and produced 17 validated
Sigma detection rules mapped to 15 distinct ATT&CK techniques, two
hypothesis-driven threat hunts and two incident case reports closed as true
positives. The baseline before this work recorded 20 assessed techniques and zero
tested detections; the final position is 21 assessed, 1 Covered, 12 Partially
Covered and 8 Not Covered.

The most important finding is a negative one, and it is reported as such. Of the
21 techniques assessed, exactly one is scored Covered, because exactly one rule has
both a confirmed malicious match and a measured benign result. The other twelve
confirmed detections fire on real attack telemetry, but their false-alarm rate is
unmeasured, because neither recording contains a baseline of ordinary business
activity. Claiming coverage without that measurement would have been easy and
would have been the exact practice this engagement exists to eliminate.

## 2. Objectives

Establish whether a documented, evidence-backed detection library and a repeatable
hunting practice can be produced from real public telemetry, and deliver the work
product of a detection engineer or threat hunter.

## 3. Scope

In scope: public telemetry analysis; Sigma rule authoring mapped to ATT&CK;
structured threat hunting; incident case reporting; response playbook authoring;
IOC research; coverage assessment; false-positive tuning; business-facing
reporting.

Out of scope per PRD section 2.9 and not built: any application, service, database
or hosted product; a live SIEM; automated response execution; production client
log access. The Python in this repository is analysis tooling, not a deliverable.

## 4. Methodology

Recorded in full in `docs/methodology.md`, which covers dataset selection,
telemetry acquisition, data preparation, field interpretation, the
detection-engineering methodology, Sigma validation, ATT&CK mapping,
threat-hunting method, IOC enrichment, incident investigation, false-positive
tuning, evidence handling, reproducibility, limitations and ethical
considerations. Four decisions dominate every result and are summarised here.

**Evidence before conclusion.** Every finding cites a record. The compliance audit
resolves all 119 event references in rules, hunts and incident reports against the
recorded dataset digests and fails if any names an unknown dataset or a line
outside that dataset's record count.

**Timestamps are not invented.** Only Sysmon `UtcTime` is treated as event-time
UTC. The four-hour discrepancy in the Security `EventTime` fields was left
unresolved. This is the direct cause of two inconclusive conclusions.

**Coverage is derived, not asserted.** `scripts/build_coverage.py` computes every
rating from the replay results. A rule with an empty declared evidence list cannot
score above Not Covered, which is what holds CT-013 and CT-014 at zero.

**Precision is measured, not assumed.** Three rules were tuned with generated
before-and-after figures. Two were tuned to zero matches because every match they
produced was benign, and both techniques therefore remain Not Covered.

## 8. Detection engineering

17 rules in `rules/`, built from the single template `rules/_template.yml`.

| Group | Count | Rules |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single-event, process creation | 5 | CT-001 Mshta inline VBScript, CT-002 encoded PowerShell, CT-003 service-launched shell, CT-004 PowerShell spawns whoami, CT-005 background PowerShell dispatcher |
| Single-event, script content | 4 | CT-006 disable script block logging, CT-007 AMSI failure flag, CT-008 decode and execute in memory, CT-009 web download with default credentials |
| Single-event, security and service | 3 | CT-010 security service discovery, CT-011 service installed with a PowerShell path, CT-012 network logon by a named account |
| Single-event, memory and share | 2 | CT-013 process access to LSASS, CT-014 admin share access by a user account |
| Correlation | 3 | CT-C01 mshta to PowerShell, CT-C02 service shell to encoded PowerShell, CT-C03 encoded PowerShell to whoami |

Every rule carries an ATT&CK technique list, a written severity justification, an
explicit covered and excluded scope, a declared evidence list, a declared negative
list, and a false-positive section naming realistic legitimate activity.

**Precision over volume.** CT-010 keys on both the `Get-Service` cmdlet and a named
product rather than matching every service enumeration. CT-002 uses a regex for the
switch boundary rather than a substring. The shipped correlation windows are 5 s,
5 s and 30 s against observed delays of 0.607 s, 0.023 s and 22.175 s, so
scheduling jitter does not suppress a real detection.

**Two rules are retained that match nothing.** CT-013 and CT-014 cover techniques
whose behaviour is absent from this telemetry. They exist so a client asking about
those techniques has something to point at. Both are held at Not Covered and
neither is presented as a detection.

## 9. Sigma validation methodology

Four classes, all recorded per rule in `detections/test-results.csv`.

1. **Syntax.** `sigma check --fail-on-issues`, warnings treated as failures. 17 of
   17 pass.
2. **Structural.** Required fields, unique UUID, at least one `attack.t` tag, a
   logsource matching the field structure, a written severity justification.
   Enforced by `scripts/prd_compliance.py`.
3. **Logical.** The condition must be expressible in the documented replay grammar.
   Parentheses, undefined selections, two string operators on one field,
   field-reference conditions and trailing operators all raise and reject the rule
   rather than silently matching nothing. All five were tested deliberately.
4. **Empirical.** Replay against the recorded telemetry with a declared evidence
   list and a declared negative list. A rule whose declared evidence does not match
   **fails**; a rule whose declared negative matches **fails**.

Correlation rules additionally assert a **window-boundary matrix**: the exact
observed delay must match and one millisecond less must not. CT-C01 fires at
0.607 s and not 0.606; CT-C02 at 0.023 s and not 0.022; CT-C03 at 22.175 s and not
22.174. A harness that ignored the window would pass these, so the assertions are
what distinguish enforced pairing from a mentioned one.

**Result: 17 rules replayed, 17 pass, 0 fail.** 15 matched declared attack
evidence. 2 match nothing by design.

## 11. Threat hunts

| Hunt | Technique | Conclusion | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HUNT-002 | T1071.001 Web Protocols | **Inconclusive** | One connection to 10.10.10.5 in a 2m14s resolved-time base; destination is RFC 1918; no response content. Pre-hunt commit `a85ce34` recorded before analysis. |
| HUNT-001 | T1021.002, T1569.002 | **Partially supported** | Service execution confirmed by a GUID join 0.578 s after the SCM reached a dynamic RPC port; lateral movement attribution inconclusive because the records naming the remote application have no resolved event time. |

HUNT-002 satisfies the PRD requirement that at least one hunt target a technique not

negative: 42 of the 43 port-80 connections in DS002 are Azure platform traffic, so
"alert on web traffic" would have been approximately 98 per cent false. That is why
T1071.001 has no rule.

## 13. Incident investigations

| Case | Host | Classification | Corroboration | Technique span |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| INC-001 | WORKSTATION6 | **True positive** | 4 independent log sources | T1543.003, T1569.002, T1059.001, T1059.003, T1033, T1685, T1027.010, T1140, T1105, T1078 |
| INC-002 | WORKSTATION5 | **True positive** | 2 independent process-creation sources | T1218.005, T1059.005, T1059.001, T1518.001 |

**INC-001.** A service named `Updater` was registered with a demand start type and
the `LocalSystem` account, and a binary path that is a command interpreter rather
than a program. The Service Control Manager became the recorded parent of a command
shell 0.578 seconds after reaching a dynamic RPC port. The PowerShell stage
disabled script block logging and forced the AMSI failure flag, then retrieved and
evaluated content from an internal address. The PowerShell module log records the
output of the attacker's own `whoami` as `nt authority\system`, so the privilege
level is **confirmed from evidence**, not inferred from a configuration field.

**INC-002.** A trusted Windows component executed an inline VBScript URL that
opened an interactive session with the `-noexit` switch, which then queried the
Sysmon service by name. Short, with no persistence and no escalation, and still a
confirmed attack.

The two cases are **not** linked into one campaign. DS001's host string and DS002's
differ, and host aliases are never merged.

The largest unresolved question in both cases is whether the acting account was
compromised. Neither recording contains a single event 4625, so there is no
authentication history at all. Both reports record this as unresolved.

## 17. Security posture

`SECURITY.md` records the threat model, nine controls asserted in code, five
defects found and fixed, and six residual risks. Current state: 8 controls pass, 1
warns, 0 fail.

The controls are runnable, which is the point: `python scripts/security_review.py`.
A clean run means the listed controls hold. It does not mean the project is free of
vulnerabilities, and it does not improve any coverage rating.

The most significant defect found was that `check_capture.py` read raw telemetry
without verifying its digest and then wrote a tracked evidence file from it. That
broke the project's core evidence contract and is now fixed.

**`scripts/sanitize.py`** produces a publishable redacted copy. 27 values masked,
0 residual on verification, and **all 52 event UIDs preserved**. The audit trail
survives redaction, which is the requirement: an audit that cannot verify its
citations is worthless.

## 18. Limitations

1. Two recordings of roughly two minutes each. Absence of evidence in a two-minute
   window is not evidence of absence, and no conclusion here relies on it except
   where that absence is itself the finding.
2. **No clean benign baseline**, which caps 12 of 21 techniques at Partially
   Covered. This is the single largest gap and the cheapest to close.
3. Unresolved event time for 1642 records, the direct cause of two inconclusive
   conclusions.
4. No proxy, gateway or packet content, which makes tool-transfer assessment
   permanently inconclusive without new collection.
5. **No authentication-failure records in either dataset**, so credential
   compromise is unanswerable in both incident cases.
6. Three techniques cannot be demonstrated at all on this host: no run-key write,
   no 4625 series, no labelled encryption sequence, and no hypervisor to generate
   one.
7. Dataset licence terms are unresolved, so external publication is gated on a
   rights review.
8. **No independent review.** Every rule, figure and conclusion was produced and
   checked by one person. This is a process gap that code quality cannot close.

## 19. Lessons learned

- **Assess across telemetry types, not one.** The Phase 4 technique assessment
  read process-creation records and found 7 candidates. Reading the recorded
  PowerShell script text produced 6 more and 2 additional techniques. A single
  telemetry type understates the data.
- **Refuse to resolve a timestamp you cannot justify.** Leaving the four-hour
  discrepancy unresolved cost two conclusions and prevented a confidently wrong
  timeline. That is a good trade.
- **A negative result is a deliverable.** HUNT-002's port-80 ratio prevented an
  entire class of bad rules and is more useful than a plausible detection would
  have been.
- **Tuning to silence is not detection.** Two tuned rules match nothing and both
  techniques remain Not Covered. Recording that honestly is more valuable than
  reporting 15 rules as covering 15 techniques.
- **Test the checker.** Five separate defects in this project's own quality tooling
  were found by running it against known-good and known-bad inputs. A validator
  that has never been shown to fail is not evidence of anything.

## 20. Conclusions

The engagement demonstrates that a defensible detection library can be built from
public telemetry, and that the honest measure of it is not rule count. Seventeen
rules were produced, fifteen confirmed against real attack telemetry, and one
technique is genuinely covered. Fifteen techniques have working rules and unmeasured
precision. Six have none, and three of those cannot be closed without telemetry
that does not exist on this host.

The recommended next actions, in order of value: record a clean benign baseline;
enable PowerShell script block logging and failed sign-in collection estate-wide;
deploy the 15 confirmed rules with their stated caveats; collect the three targeted
recordings; enable web gateway logging.
